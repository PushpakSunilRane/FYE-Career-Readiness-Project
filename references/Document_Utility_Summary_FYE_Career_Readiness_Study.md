### 3.1 Master Student Employment Agreement (Career Center) — campus benchmark

**What it is:** Career Center Student Employment Agreement outlining expectations, professionalism, confidentiality/FERPA, dress code, timesheets, mandatory monthly staff meetings/trainings, and progressive discipline.

**Especially relevant passages:**

- Explicit developmental mission: developing student employees’ potential for post-college careers.
- **Mid-semester and end-of-semester check-ins** reviewing strengths, support needs, and expectations.
- On-the-job + peer training model; monthly paid meetings as shared learning time.
- Professionalism framed as customer service, confidentiality, attendance, and modeling workplace norms for other students.

**How to use it:**

- **Comparative analysis:** Career Center vs FYE student-employment developmental systems (check-ins frequency, competency language, feedback formality).
- **Shared campus language** for professionalism, confidentiality, and performance management.
- **Instrument inspiration:** mid/end check-in structure can inform interview guides for supervisors and student employees.
- **Literature / local context section:** show that Chico State student employment is already framed as career preparation, not only operational labor.

**Limitation:** Career Center–specific (not FYE). Use as comparison/benchmark, not as FYE’s exact protocol. Evaluation categories are more office/customer-service oriented than FYE’s research-coordinator model.

### 3.2 Student Assistant Evaluation Sheet — ready-to-adapt assessment instrument

**What it is:** Excel supervisor evaluation form with a 1–4 scale:

`1 = Unsatisfactory | 2 = Needs Improvement | 3 = Meets Expectations | 4 = Exceeds Expectations`

**Competency domains currently measured:**

1. **Professionalism** — knowledge of environment; staff interaction; communication; confidentiality; attire
2. **Customer Service** — greeting; demeanor; respect/patience; phone etiquette; employer interactions
3. **Performance** — accuracy/detail; initiative; following directions; messages; workflow proficiency; seeking additional tasks
4. **Work Habits** — attendance; punctuality; schedule awareness; flexibility; shift handoff; productivity

**Qualitative / reflective prompts (student voice):**

1. What do you like best about your job?
2. What do you like least / challenges you most?
3. Are there skills you would like to use and/or develop?
4. Summer availability
5. Availability/plans next semester

Also includes supervisor comments + dual signatures (acknowledgment, not necessarily agreement).

**How to use it:**


| Use case                       | Application                                                                            |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Baseline instrument analysis   | Code domains against NACE / WEF / FYE competencies                                     |
| Rubric redesign                | Keep 1–4 scale; remap items to FYE competencies + career readiness                     |
| Mixed-methods design           | Quantitative ratings + open-ended skill development prompts                            |
| Pre/post or mid/end comparison | Reuse structure for new vs returning employees                                         |
| Goal-setting link              | Prompt #3 directly supports guided professional goal-setting                           |
| Alumni adaptation              | Reword prompts to “skills used after graduation,” “skills wished developed more,” etc. |


**Crosswalk opportunity (example):**

- Evaluation “Communicates well…” ↔ NACE Communication / WEF empathy & active listening  
- “Demonstrates initiative…” ↔ NACE Career & Self-Development / WEF motivation & self-awareness  
- “Accuracy and attention to detail” ↔ Dependability / attention to detail  
- “Looks for additional tasks…” ↔ Initiative, leadership potential

**Limitation:** Tuned to Career Center/SEO front-desk work. For FYE, replace phone/employer items with research support, peer coaching, competency feedback quality, facilitation, etc., while preserving the useful structure.

### 3.3 Career Handbook (Chico State Career Center, Vol. 25) — intervention & teaching toolkit

**What it is:** 26-page campus career handbook covering job search, LinkedIn, AI use, employer-sought skills, resumes, CVs, cover letters, career fair/elevator pitch, and interviewing.

**Highest-value sections for this project:**

#### A. NACE Career Readiness Competencies (page on “What Employers Seek”)

Employers’ rated importance of career readiness competencies (handbook figures), including:

- Communication  
- Critical Thinking  
- Teamwork  
- Professionalism  
- Equity & Inclusion  
- Technology  
- Career & Self Development  
- Leadership

This is the best **local teaching framework** to align FYE weekly competencies with a nationally recognized career-readiness model.

#### B. Skill-statement method (transferable skills → evidence)

Formula taught:
`Power/Action Verb + Transferable Skill + Task/Result`

Levels of skill statements (basic → advanced → advanced with results) are ideal for:

- Weekly reflection quality rubrics  
- Helping students turn Work Package experiences into resume/interview evidence  
- Training leads who coach Research Coordinators

#### C. Interview support (directly named in the study)

Interviewing Guidelines include:

- Common questions and strong-answer strategies  
- Employer questions students should ask  
- Before / during / after interview checklist  
- **STAR method** (Situation–Task–Action–Result)  
- Sample behavioral questions (customer conflict, initiative, analysis, creativity, high pressure, etc.)

Also includes AI prompts for practicing behavioral/situational interview questions—useful for standardized interview-support workshops.

#### D. Goal-setting / career planning supports

- AI prompts for career action plans and major/career exploration  
- Emphasis that goals/values should guide decisions (not tools alone)  
- Networking, informational interviews, mentors, Handshake/LinkedIn pathways

**How to use it:**

1. **Curriculum for interview support workshops** during huddles, UNIV 389, or mid-semester PD.
2. **Weekly reflection scaffold:** ask students to write one STAR story per targeted competency.
3. **Alignment matrix:** map FYE Work Package competencies ↔ NACE 8 competencies ↔ handbook skill statements.
4. **Alumni survey design:** ask which handbook-style skills (communication, teamwork, etc.) proved most valuable post-graduation.
5. **Training Lead coaching script:** use skill-statement levels when giving competency feedback.

**Limitation:** General career education resource; not FYE-specific outcome data. Treat as intervention content + framework, not as evidence of FYE impact.

### 3.4 NACE Job Outlook 2026 Spring Update — employer demand evidence (U.S. college hiring)

**What it is:** NACE survey update (fielded Feb 12–Mar 17, 2026; ~185 employer respondents) on Class of 2026 hiring, AI, and skills/attributes employers seek.

**Findings most useful to the study:**

#### Hiring context

- Employers project about a **5.6% increase** in hiring for the Class of 2026.
- Majority maintaining or increasing hires; talent-pipeline / succession planning is a major reason for increasing hires.

#### Skills & attributes employers seek on resumes

High-priority attributes include teamwork, communication, problem-solving, initiative, analytical skills, adaptability, leadership potential, detail orientation, technical/computer skills, and (rising) AI-related skills.  
*(Use handbook + NACE together; verify exact percentages from charts when citing in formal writing.)*

#### Critical methodological finding for FYE assessment design

Employers strongly prefer candidates who **demonstrate skills with examples**, not merely list them:

- Listing specific skills **with examples** is the dominant preference (~59% in extracted results).
- Listing skills without examples is far less valued.

This directly validates FYE’s weekly reflection + feedback model: students should practice producing **evidence-based skill narratives**.

#### AI skills as an emerging readiness dimension

- Demand for AI skills in entry-level work is rising.
- Employers seek abilities such as identifying appropriate AI tools, prompting effectively, using AI for productivity, and reviewing/revising AI outputs.
- Many employers assign AI-related intern projects; conversations about AI replacing early-career tasks are happening, though complete replacement is not the majority view.

**How to use it:**

- **Literature / rationale section:** why competency reflection with examples matters for hireability.
- **Competency framework update:** consider adding AI literacy / ethical AI use as an optional FYE competency.
- **Interview support content:** teach students to convert weekly Work Package entries into resume bullets and behavioral answers.
- **Alumni instrument:** ask whether graduates can show examples of teamwork, communication, problem-solving, etc., from FYE work.
- **Benchmarking:** compare FYE-emphasized competencies with national employer priorities; identify gaps (e.g., analytical/quantitative, AI, leadership).

**Limitation:** National employer sample, not Chico State–specific; Spring Update is shorter than the full Job Outlook report. Still excellent for external validity of skill priorities.

### 3.5 WEF Future of Jobs Report 2025 — long-horizon skills taxonomy

**What it is:** World Economic Forum insight report (Jan 2025) based on perspectives from 1,000+ employers (14M+ workers; 22 industry clusters; 55 economies), projecting 2025–2030 skill and job transformation.

**Key findings for FYE career readiness:**

#### Core skills employers identify as essential now

Top core skills emphasize a blend of cognitive + self-efficacy + interpersonal skills, including:

- Analytical thinking (most cited core skill; ~7 in 10 employers)
- Resilience, flexibility, and agility
- Leadership and social influence
- Creative thinking
- Motivation and self-awareness
- Technological literacy
- Empathy and active listening
- Curiosity and lifelong learning
- Talent management
- Service orientation / customer service

#### Fastest-growing skills to 2030

- AI and big data  
- Networks and cybersecurity  
- Technological literacy  
- Creative thinking  
- Resilience, flexibility, and agility  
- Curiosity and lifelong learning  
- Leadership and social influence  
- Talent management  
- Analytical thinking  
- Environmental stewardship (newly prominent)

#### Why this matters for a student-employment development program

- ~**39%** of workers’ existing skill sets expected to transform/become outdated by 2030 (skill instability slowing vs prior editions).
- Skill gaps are the top barrier to business transformation (~63% of employers).
- Large-scale upskilling/reskilling need: if workforce = 100 people, ~59 need training by 2030.
- Human-centered skills remain essential alongside tech skills—supports FYE’s reflection, mentoring, leadership, and service practices.

**How to use it:**

1. **Future-proof the FYE competency list** (add/strengthen lifelong learning, resilience/adaptability, leadership/social influence, tech/AI literacy).
2. **Justify weekly reflection as lifelong-learning practice**, not only semester evaluation.
3. **Frame Training Lead mentoring** as early practice in talent management / leadership and social influence.
4. **Alumni survey constructs:** perceived growth in WEF-aligned skills; preparedness for skill disruption.
5. **Discussion section:** locate FYE within global upskilling discourse (campus micro-upskilling system).

**Limitation:** Global/macro; not U.S. higher-ed student employment specific. Use for framework and foresight, not as direct local outcome proof.

## 5. Concrete uses by study component

### 5.1 Weekly competency reflection

**Primary sources:** FYE Contract; Career Handbook skill-statement + STAR methods; WEF “curiosity & lifelong learning.”

**What to do:**

- Document the existing Practice Plan / Self-Report fields.
- Build a reflection quality rubric (lists skill vs gives example vs shows result).
- Optionally add one weekly prompt: “Which NACE/WEF skill did you practice, and what is your STAR evidence?”

### 5.2 Assessment and feedback

**Primary sources:** FYE Contract; Student Assistant Evaluation Sheet; Employment Agreement check-ins; NACE “demonstrate with examples.”

**What to do:**

- Analyze frequency/quality of written Work Package feedback.
- Adapt evaluation sheet into an FYE mid-semester + end-semester rubric aligned to FYE competencies.
- Compare new vs returning employees on rating trajectories and feedback themes.
- Code qualitative comments for professionalism, initiative, communication, leadership, etc.

### 5.3 Guided professional goal-setting

**Primary sources:** Evaluation prompt on skills to develop; Contract 1:1s; Handbook career action-plan guidance; WEF motivation & self-awareness.

**What to do:**

- Treat weekly Practice Plans + 1:1 notes as goal-setting artifacts.
- Create a simple goal taxonomy (skill goal, performance goal, career/interview goal).
- Measure goal quality (specific, skill-linked, evidence-based) and progress at mid/exit.

### 5.4 Interview support

**Primary sources:** Career Handbook interviewing/STAR section; NACE employer preferences; FYE rehire/exit interview touchpoint.

**What to do:**

- Design a short interview-prep module for student employees using handbook questions + STAR.
- Use FYE weekly experiences as raw material for behavioral answers.
- For alumni: ask which FYE stories they used in real interviews and whether they felt prepared.

### 5.5 Multi-cohort data collection

**Primary sources:** Verification Letter populations; Contract role distinctions; Evaluation sheet dual quantitative/qualitative structure.

**Suggested cohort uses of documents:**


| Cohort                         | Best document-informed approach                                                                                        |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **New employees**              | Baseline competency self-assessment (handbook NACE list) + weekly fidelity + mid/end eval                              |
| **Returning employees**        | Compare growth/retention of practices; leadership tasks (Training Lead pathway); mentoring load                        |
| **Past employees / post-grad** | Retrospective survey mapped to NACE/WEF skills; interview-story use; perceived FYE contribution to first-job readiness |

## 9. Bottom line

These documents collectively support the project in three complementary ways:

1. **Describe the program practices to be assessed** (especially the FYE contract’s weekly Practice Plan / Self-Report / feedback cycle, 1:1s, mid-eval, and exit/rehire interviews).
2. **Provide assessment and intervention tools** (evaluation sheet structure; handbook skill-statement and STAR interview methods; goal-oriented prompts).
3. **Supply external evidence and competency language** (NACE career readiness + Job Outlook employer priorities; WEF future skills) so findings can be framed as improving student employees’ real career readiness—not only internal job performance.

Used together, they are sufficient to design the study’s conceptual framework, instrumentation, interview-support curriculum, and alumni survey constructs. They are **not yet sufficient alone** as outcome evidence; the next research step is to collect Work Package, evaluation, and multi-cohort survey/interview data against the frameworks these documents provide.
